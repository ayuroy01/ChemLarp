ChemLarp is **free and open source** under the **GNU Affero General Public License, version 3** (AGPL-3.0-only).
It is also available under a **commercial licence** for organisations that cannot meet the AGPL's terms.

*This page explains the licences in plain words; the licence texts themselves are what apply.*

## Using ChemLarp: you can, for free

- Students, teachers, researchers, companies and anyone else may **use** ChemLarp for any purpose: in the
  browser, the apps, the command line or from Python, including at work and in commercial research.
- You may **study and change** the source code, and **share** copies, changed or not.
- Results you compute with ChemLarp (energies, structures, reports) are yours. The AGPL does not cover them.

## What the AGPL asks in return

If you **distribute** ChemLarp or a program built on it, or let people **use a modified version over a network**
(for example, as a website or web service), you must:

1. license your whole combined program under the AGPL-3.0, and give its users the **complete source code**;
2. keep the copyright notices, the `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` files, and say what you changed;
3. not add restrictions beyond the AGPL.

In short: anyone can build on ChemLarp, but they cannot turn it into a closed, proprietary product.

## Commercial licence

If you want to include ChemLarp in a closed-source product or service, or cannot release your source under the
AGPL, a commercial licence from the copyright holder removes those obligations. To ask, open an issue titled
"Commercial licence" at https://github.com/ayuroy01/ChemLarp/issues, or contact
[ayuroy01 on GitHub](https://github.com/ayuroy01).

## Name and logo

"ChemLarp" and the ChemLarp logo identify this project and its author. The AGPL covers the code, not the name.

- You **may** say that your work is "based on ChemLarp" or "uses ChemLarp", with a link to this repository.
- You **may not** call a modified version or a different product "ChemLarp", use the logo for it, or suggest that
  it is official or endorsed, without written permission.
- Forks must use a different name and logo.
